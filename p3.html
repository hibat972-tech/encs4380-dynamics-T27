Digital PID

A microcontroller does not control continuously. Every \(T_s\) seconds it reads the sensor, computes one number, and holds that number on the output until the next sample. The plant keeps moving in between. Make \(T_s\) larger and watch the response break.

Theory in two minutes

The digital PID (assignment equation)

\[ u[k]=K_p\,e[k]+K_iT_s\sum_{j=0}^{k}e[j]+K_d\,\frac{e[k]-e[k-1]}{T_s} \]

The integral becomes a running sum, the derivative becomes a difference. At \(k=0\) the previous error is \(e[-1]=0\), so the first sample has a short derivative “kick” when the setpoint jumps.

Sample and hold

\(u\) is computed at \(t=kT_s\) and held (zero-order hold) until \(t=(k+1)T_s\). The plant is integrated with a much smaller RK4 step in between, so the plant stays continuous and only the controller is digital. The control signal is a staircase.

Why large \(T_s\) hurts

The controller is blind between samples, and the hold adds an average delay of about \(T_s/2\). For P control on a first-order plant the closed loop is stable only while \(\left|e^{-T_s/\tau}-KK_p\left(1-e^{-T_s/\tau}\right)\right|<1\). For \(K=1,\ \tau=1,\ K_p=9\) that means \(T_s<0.223\) s.

Saturation and anti-windup

An actuator has limits. While \(u\) is stuck at a limit the error stays large, and the sum keeps growing (windup). When the output finally arrives, the stored sum is huge and drives it far past the setpoint. Anti-windup here stops adding to the sum while the output is saturated and the error would push it further into the limit.

What to notice

  • Start with Ts = 0.01 s: the staircase is so fine that the response looks continuous. Raise Ts: the steps widen and the output lags behind.
  • Ts = 0.9 s breaks this loop. u flips between the limits and the output never settles. The sweep plots show where it starts.
  • Windup preset: compare the overshoot with anti-windup OFF and ON. The only difference is one line in the controller.
  • The control signal never leaves the red limits, but the grey “asked-for” value in P1 can. Here the code does the same as the Arduino: constrain().

Engineering meaning

  • Pick Ts about 10–20 times faster than the closed-loop response you want. Slower sampling needs lower gains.
  • Using micros() and a fixed period keeps Ts constant. A varying Ts changes the effective Ki and Kd.
  • Anti-windup matters whenever the setpoint can jump farther than the actuator can follow, such as a speed command that is larger than the motor can reach right away.
