A slow sensor in the loop

A controller never sees the real output. It sees what the sensor reports, and a sensor from Homework 1 reports a delayed, smoothed version. The controller reacts to old news, and that is enough to make a well-tuned loop overshoot and then oscillate.

Theory in two minutes

The measured signal

\[ \tau_s\,\dot y_m + y_m = y,\qquad e = r - y_m \]

The sensor is the first-order model of Homework 1 with time constant \(\tau_s\). The error is computed from \(y_m\), not from the true output \(y\). In steady state \(y_m=y\), so a sensor lag changes how the loop gets there, not where it ends up.

Why the loop rings

With the plant \(K/(\tau s+1)\) and a sensor \(1/(\tau_s s+1)\), the loop already has two lags. P control gives \(\tau\tau_s s^2+(\tau+\tau_s)s+1+KK_p=0\), whose damping ratio \(\zeta=\dfrac{\tau+\tau_s}{2\sqrt{\tau\tau_s(1+KK_p)}}\) falls as \(\tau_s\) grows.

Why PI can go unstable

Adding \(K_i\) gives a third-order loop: \(\tau\tau_s s^3+(\tau+\tau_s)s^2+(1+KK_p)s+KK_i=0\). It is stable only while

\[ (\tau+\tau_s)(1+KK_p) > \tau\,\tau_s\,KK_i . \]

When \(\tau_s\) passes this limit the oscillation grows. The control limit then holds it at a steady swing.

The practical lesson

If a loop oscillates, look at the sensor before blaming the gains. A sensor should be several times faster than the process it measures. Otherwise the controller has to be detuned (lower \(K_i\) and \(K_p\)), and the loop gets slower.

What to notice

  • Press Fast sensor, then Slow sensor. The orange curve now trails the blue one, and the blue curve overshoots much further, because the controller keeps pushing while the sensor still says “not there yet”.
  • Press Very slow sensor. The loop never settles. u swings between its limits.
  • With P only, the loop only rings (it always settles), and the steady-state error stays 10% whatever τs is.
  • Sensors with τs close to the plant’s τ need much lower gains. Try Ki = 1 with τs = 2.5 s.

Engineering meaning

  • For the Micromouse speed loop, the “sensor” is the encoder speed estimate. If it is averaged over a long window, it behaves like a lag, and the speed loop will ring.
  • A rule of thumb: the sensor should respond at least 5–10 times faster than the closed loop you want. Otherwise filter less, sample faster, or tune the loop slower.
