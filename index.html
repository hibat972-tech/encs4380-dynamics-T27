Dynamic Characteristics Interactive Toolkit

A sensor never tells you what is happening now. It tells you what happened a moment ago, smoothed out. Static characteristics (sensitivity, linearity) describe a sensor once everything has settled. Dynamic characteristics describe what it does while the input is changing: how far behind it is, and whether that delay will break your measurement.

Try it on the right. A thermometer moves from room air at 20 °C into tea at 80 °C. Move the reading time and see how wrong a perfectly calibrated sensor can be.

Validation status

The six checks from Section 3.2 of the assignment are computed live, in your browser, by the same functions that draw the plots. The Validation page shows the expected value, the calculated value and the result for each one.

Suggested 10-minute demo route

  1. Home (1 min): the thermometer story. Read it at τ, then at 3τ.
  2. Module 1 (2 min): the 63.2% and 95% markers, then the ramp lag, then the straight log line.
  3. Module 2 (2 min): the cut-off button, then the 100 Hz / 5% design in the calculator.
  4. Module 3 (2.5 min): the three damping cases, then m = 1, b = 2, k = 4 with eigenvalues equal to the poles.
  5. Module 4 (1.5 min): play one round live.
  6. Validation (1 min): all six checks pass.

Key symbols

\(K\)Static sensitivityoutput per inputHow much output you get per unit of input once everything has settled.
\(\tau\)Time constantsHow slow a first-order sensor is: 63.2% of a step after τ, 95% after 3τ.
\(M,\ \phi\)Amplitude ratio, phase—, degreesHow much smaller and how much later a sine comes out.
\(\delta=M-1\)Dynamic error— or %The fractional amplitude error caused by the sensor's dynamics.
\(\omega_c=1/\tau\)Cut-off frequencyrad/sWhere the output is down to 70.7% (−3 dB) and lags by 45°.
\(\zeta\)Damping ratio—The shape of a second-order response: overshoot or no overshoot.
\(\omega_n\)Natural frequencyrad/sThe speed of a second-order response. All times scale with \(1/\omega_n\).
\(t_r,\ t_p,\ M_p,\ t_s\)Rise, peak, overshoot, settlings, s, %, sNumbers read off a step response.
\(\mathbf A,\ \mathbf B\)State and input matrices—The model written as \(\dot{\mathbf x}=\mathbf A\mathbf x+\mathbf BF\). The eigenvalues of A are the poles.
