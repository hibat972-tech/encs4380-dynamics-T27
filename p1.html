Close the loop around a Homework 1 plant and see what each term of a PID controller does. Everything below is a numerical simulation of the loop (RK4), recomputed every time a slider moves.

Theory in two minutes

The loop

\[ e = r - y_m,\qquad u = K_p\,e + K_i\!\int e\,dt + K_d\,\frac{de}{dt} \]

The controller compares the setpoint \(r\) with the measured output \(y_m\) and drives the plant with \(u\). The actuator can only deliver a limited range, \(u_{min}\le u\le u_{max}\). Here the derivative acts on the measurement, which equals \(de/dt\) for a constant setpoint but avoids an infinite spike when the setpoint jumps.

First-order plant, closed form

P only: \(\tau_{cl}=\dfrac{\tau}{1+KK_p}\), and \(e_{ss}=\dfrac{1}{1+KK_p}\).

PI: \(\tau s^2+(1+KK_p)s+KK_i=0\), so \(\omega_n=\sqrt{KK_i/\tau}\) and \(\zeta=\dfrac{1+KK_p}{2\sqrt{\tau KK_i}}\).

PID: the first coefficient becomes \(\tau+KK_d\).

D adds damping to a plant that can ring (second order). On a first-order plant it only adds inertia, because the closed loop has \(\tau+KK_d\). Try both.

How the numbers are measured

All four are read off the simulated curve. Rise time: 10→90% of the final value \(y_\infty\). Overshoot: \(100\,(y_{peak}-y_\infty)/y_\infty\). Settling time: the last time the output is outside ±2% of \(y_\infty\). Steady-state error: \(r-y_\infty\), with \(y_\infty\) the mean of the last 10% of the run. If the output never settles the page says so instead of showing a number.

Where u comes from: the P, I and D parts

u is the sum of the three curves (before the limit). Watch the I part grow while an error remains, and the D part react only while the output is moving.

Closed-loop equation for the current settings

What to notice

  • P only: raise Kp and the response speeds up, but a steady-state error 1/(1 + K·Kp) remains. With Kp = 9 it is 10%.
  • Add I: the error goes to zero. The price is overshoot, because the integral keeps pushing after the output arrives.
  • Add D (second-order plant): the ringing is damped. Compare the overshoot with Kd = 0 and Kd = 0.6.
  • Lower the limits: u cannot follow the controller, the rise slows down, and the grey dotted curve shows what was asked for.

Engineering meaning

  • Most real loops start as PI. D is added only when the plant rings or the response must be calmed, and it amplifies sensor noise.
  • With a limit on u, a large integral can wind up. Turn anti-windup off and compare the overshoot. P3 studies this in the digital controller.
  • For a motor speed loop, K and τ come from a PWM step test. The My System page does that for the project motor.
